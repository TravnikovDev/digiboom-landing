Etsy and Shopify are not competitors for your business. They are two halves of one
strategy, and running both is the one move that pays back most for a digital
seller who has outgrown one shop.

Here is why the pairing works, and the exact thing that makes people abandon it.

## What each one is for

**Etsy is a marketplace.** Its job is putting your work in front of people who have never
heard of you. Millions of buyers arrive there already shopping, and Etsy's search decides
who they see. You pay listing and transaction fees for that introduction.

**Shopify is your own store.** Nobody arrives by accident. But there is no cut taken on
discovery, no algorithm to please, no risk of a suspension email ending your month, and
the customer is *yours*: their email, their repeat purchases, the relationship.

Discovery and ownership. You want both, and neither platform gives you the other.

## What running both looks like in practice

The healthy version:

1. **Etsy stays the front door.** Keep it optimised. It is where strangers find you.
2. **Shopify becomes the home.** Same catalog, your branding, your rules, full margin.
3. **Everything you sell exists in both places**: the customer chooses where to buy.
4. **You point people home when you can.** The card in the download, the email footer,
   your social bio: all pointing at the store you own.

Over time, the mix shifts. Early on almost all sales come from Etsy's traffic. As your
audience grows, more of it arrives directly, at full margin. That shift is the whole
game.

## And then the duplicate work eats you

Here is where this plan usually dies.

Two platforms means everything is done twice. Every product uploaded twice, with its
files, descriptions, variants, tags and licences reshaped for each platform's rules.
Every price change made twice. Every new version uploaded twice. Every typo fixed twice.

It compounds: a fifty-product catalog on two platforms is a hundred listings to keep
straight. Add a third platform and it is a hundred and fifty.

So one of three things happens. The Shopify store goes stale, quietly, until it is
embarrassing. Or the seller spends hours a week on data entry that produces nothing new.
Or they give up and go back to one shop, and the ceiling comes back with them.

None of that is a discipline failure. It is a tooling gap.

## What syncing should actually mean

Real sync means: **you change something once, and everywhere catches up.**

- Ship a new version of a file → every store has the new file.
- Change a price → every store reprices.
- Fix a description → fixed everywhere.
- Open a *fourth* platform → your catalog is already there when it opens.

That is the difference between "I sell on two platforms" being a strategy and being a
chore you resent.
